# Noridoc: 3D sprite renderer

Path: @/tools/sprites

### Overview

- Renders the game's generated world art from small 3D models with a deterministic software rasterizer; generation runs at build time and does not require a browser or GPU.
- Produces PNG and JSON sprite definitions for [tools/art](../art/README.md), which prepares and packs them into the runtime atlas and manifest. Generated rock keys replace the prepared rock surface and add directional cliff faces.
- `pnpm art:prepare` calls `renderSprites`, which writes every sprite from `buildSprites()` into `public/sprites/`. `pnpm sprites:render` only writes contact sheets under `docs/art/` for visual review.
- `renderUiIcons` in [ui-icons.ts](ui-icons.ts) writes registered UI icons at native 16px and 24px sizes under `public/sprites/ui-icons/`. Most ride and object icons use the deterministic `renderMesh` rasterizer; the hand-authored icon set uses pixel shapes directly so its silhouette and shading remain legible at the UI's native size. Both paths share the icon registry and generated asset locations. UI icons are generated after world sprites during `art:prepare` and are intentionally excluded from the world atlas.

### How it fits into the larger codebase

- [generate.ts](generate.ts) builds the sprite definitions consumed by [tools/art](../art/README.md); the asset preparation step clears generated output before rendering and packing, so each run creates one canonical set of sprite keys. [prepared-assets.ts](../art/prepared-assets.ts) supplies the shared key lists for source-sheet terrain, props, and UI sprites to both preparation and coverage reporting.
- [src/render/world-art.ts](../../src/render/world-art.ts) selects runtime keys and frames from ride type, state, camera direction, and tick. [src/render/coaster-art.ts](../../src/render/coaster-art.ts) shares coaster family, track-piece, vehicle-pose, and key conventions with generation.
- Both [renderers](../../src/render/docs.md) read the packed manifest. The `native` metadata emitted by [output.ts](output.ts) keeps rendered sprites at their source frame size and aligns their anchor to the projected world point; raw textures such as water are mapped by the renderer.
- [generate.test.ts](generate.test.ts) checks generated key coverage and model-specific sprite sets against simulation catalogs.
- [coverage.ts](coverage.ts) inventories renderable catalog and world variants against keys emitted by [generate.ts](generate.ts) and the prepared-source registry. Backend coverage is derived from whether each row's required keys are available; missing keys are reported as placeholder fallback. `pnpm art:coverage` writes the checked-in [coverage table](../../docs/art/coverage.md), and the Vite development server exposes the same live inventory at `/__art-coverage`.

### Core Implementation

- [raster.ts](raster.ts), [mesh.ts](mesh.ts), and [palette.ts](palette.ts) define the dimetric projection, mesh operations, z-buffered shading, outlines, and fixed palette used by the model modules.
- [models/paths.ts](models/paths.ts) generates connection-mask and ramp variants. [models/water.ts](models/water.ts) generates palette-cycle texture frames. Their keys are consumed by the path and water paths in [src/render](../../src/render/docs.md).
- [models/path-additions.ts](models/path-additions.ts) supplies four pre-rendered views for rain shelters, intact and vandalised benches, bins, and lamps. Rain shelters are emitted as raw boxed art and drawn in the shared shelter box. Benches, bins, and lamps are native sprites drawn at frame size on their ground pivot, so rotating the camera never rescales them. Models are authored for the open edge facing +x: a bench puts its backrest toward +x (the grass) and seats into the path, a bin carries a hinge bar on its outer half, and lamps are rotationally symmetric upright posts with a lantern head. The vandalised variants keep the same silhouette with a snapped seat or backrest plank, a fallen bin lid, or a dimmed lamp head with a shard on the ground. [models/litter.ts](models/litter.ts) supplies four views for trash and vomit. [models/flower-bed.ts](models/flower-bed.ts) generates the four flower colours as native one-tile sprites; soil follows the tile's two terrain triangles, with a narrow low edge and compact flower crowns above it.
- [models/ride-catalog.ts](models/ride-catalog.ts) supplies flat-ride models and catalog footprints. Each ride has four camera views; animated gentle and thrill rides include an idle frame and operating frames. [models/facilities/index.ts](models/facilities/index.ts), [models/stations.ts](models/stations.ts), and [models/park-entrance.ts](models/park-entrance.ts) generate stall/facility fronts, direction-aware entrance and exit huts, and the park gate. Station huts use category-specific post, roof, and sign palettes and a one-tile footprint; their current scale targets about one tile in width and roughly twice a guest's height as an approximate art direction guide, not a verified RCT2 measurement.
- [models/people.ts](models/people.ts) generates compact, slim guest and staff figures with a small rounded head, narrow torso, clear clothing and trouser colors, and four walking poses. [generate.ts](generate.ts) renders guest sprites for each tint, cardinal direction, and pose, and staff sprites for each uniform role, direction, and pose. These frames are emitted at their model-rendered native size and enter the same atlas preparation path as other generated world art.
- Flat gentle and thrill rides (Merry-Go-Round 3x3, Twist 3x3, Haunted House 3x3, Top Spin 3x3, Enterprise 4x4, Swinging Ship 1x5, Ferris Wheel 1x4, Spiral Slide 2x2, Observation Tower 1x1) are authored at their real footprint size in [models/flat-rides.ts](models/flat-rides.ts), in physical tile units around the centre of the footprint's bounding box (a seated guest is about 0.3 tall, a standing one 0.56). [models/rides/](models/rides/common.ts) wraps each with its rider seats for the eight operating poses; the idle pose is `build(-1)`. [ride-cells.ts](ride-cells.ts) cuts every pose into tile-sized slices (see Things to Know).
- `rideFitScale` in [generate.ts](generate.ts) still enlarges the single-tile Go-Karts circuit (at most 1.3x, never shrinking, never wider than the tile diamond); flat rides with a footprint are not scaled.
- [models/go-karts.ts](models/go-karts.ts) supplies the detailed circuit and four kart poses used for Go-Karts, while other flat rides keep the selected models from the flat-ride catalog.
- [models/coaster/](models/coaster/sprites.ts) produces coaster track cells, supports, stations, and cars under family-specific keys. Track coverage follows each ride family's permitted simulation pieces; car variants encode heading, pitch, and bank to match [src/worker/car-poses.ts](../../src/worker/car-poses.ts).
- [models/trees.ts](models/trees.ts) supplies the palm and cactus ambient trees (`trees/tree-palm`, `trees/tree-cactus`), which are registered in [generate.ts](generate.ts) alongside the source-sheet trees and rendered by `pnpm art:prepare`. Their colours come from the existing green, wood and pink ramps.
- [generate.ts](generate.ts) also emits raw tiled rock textures: an opaque surface and four direction-specific cliff faces under `terrain/rock/`. These definitions run after terrain-sheet preparation and replace those prepared keys in generated output; the renderers consume them through the shared world atlas.
- [models/transport.ts](models/transport.ts), the transport ride models, and [models/transport-track.ts](models/transport-track.ts) generate transport track cells and vehicles. The transport atlas includes flat pieces and the small left and right turns used by normal circuits; larger turns, banking, steep slopes, and looping pieces remain outside this shared atlas set. [models/water-track.ts](models/water-track.ts) generates water-ride track cells, splash-drop art, and boat poses for Log Flume and River Rapids. Water-channel surfaces and walls follow each piece's sampled track geometry; boat sprites are generated for each heading, pitch, and packed passenger count from zero to seven, supplying empty and loaded variants. The Log Flume model draws up to four passenger figures per boat, while River Rapids draws up to seven per raft. [generate.ts](generate.ts) derives water track coverage from each ride's allowed-piece catalog and registers the family-specific sprites. The older animated water-ride dioramas remain generated for their existing keys.

### Things to Know

- Track frames ([models/coaster/frames.ts](models/coaster/frames.ts)) follow the simulation's left-handed axes: heading +x has its right-hand side at +y and up is forward x right. Bank angles from the sample table therefore lower the inside rail of a banked turn, and the car sprites use the same convention. Frames are built from the stored sample tangents, transported along the piece with the accumulated twist spread out so both ends match the cross-section the neighbouring piece starts with (the vertical loop instead keeps its plane square to +y). A piece's end frame therefore equals the next piece's first frame.
- [track-compose.ts](track-compose.ts) lays pieces end to end with the simulation's exit rule and draws their cell sprites the way the renderer does: atlas trim, anchor shift, projection and painter order. [track-seams.test.ts](track-seams.test.ts) checks that every connectable piece pair meets within 0.01 tiles, that banked turns lean inwards, that inversions start and end upright, that cell sprites add up to the unclipped model within one pixel for every coaster, water and transport family in all four views, and that supports stand under the rails. `pnpm sprites:track-sheet` writes `docs/art/track-circuit-steel.png` (the e2e steel showcase circuit in four rotations) and `docs/art/track-pieces-steel.png` (each piece between its matching straights).
- [generate.test.ts](generate.test.ts) verifies that both small turn directions produce opaque pixels for each transport style in every camera view. This guards the track-piece subset shared by Miniature Railway, Monorail, Chairlift, and Junior Coaster against falling back to the renderer's procedural marker on ordinary circuits.

- [tools/art/categories.ts](../art/categories.ts) maps keys to separately packed atlas categories. People sprites are packed into the eager `people` category, while flat rides, stalls, facilities, transport ride art, transport tracks, vehicles, water rides, and coasters load lazily. `rides/coaster/<family>/...` and `rides/water/<family>/...` keys map to lazy family pages. Track art under `track/<style>/...` is grouped by style. The key prefix is therefore part of the runtime loading contract.
- [coverage.test.ts](coverage.test.ts) ties generated and prepared-key coverage to simulation catalogs, verifies every prepared catalog key and both UI icon sizes are available, rejects ride rows without required keys, and checks that missing art is reported as placeholder fallback. [generate.test.ts](generate.test.ts) checks four distinct camera views and validates the rain shelter's one-tile footprint and broad canopy silhouette. The inventory records pixel-art icons, procedural/CSS effects and placement overlays separately from sprite-backed world content.
- [ui-icons.test.ts](ui-icons.test.ts) checks the hand-authored UI icon silhouettes at native size and verifies their pixel bounds and palette variety. The icon shapes are source data; `art:prepare` regenerates the PNGs consumed by [src/ui/icons.ts](../../src/ui/icons.ts).
- Coaster keys use `rides/coaster/<family>/track/...`, `support...`, `stations/...`, and `vehicles/...`; water track and boat keys use `rides/water/<family>/track/...` and `rides/water/<family>/vehicles/...`; transport track and vehicle keys use `track/<style>/...` and `vehicles/<style>/...`. Flat ride animations use `rides/flat/<slug>/view-<view>` and facility sprites use `stalls/<slug>/facing-<facing>/view-<view>`.
- Flat-ride slice contract: `rides/flat/<slug>/view-<w>/cell-<i>` where `i` indexes the unrotated `RIDE_CATALOG[type].footprint` and `w = (cameraRotation + rideDir) & 3` (`rideCellSpriteKey` in [world-art.ts](../../src/render/world-art.ts)). The sim's `rotateTile` turns footprint offsets by the ride's `dir` and the camera turns them again in the same sense, so the model is rotated `w` quarter turns and the slice is the part over tile `rotatePoint(footprint[i], w)`, re-centred on that tile. [ride-cells.test.ts](ride-cells.test.ts) proves for every ride, dir 0-3 and camera 0-3 that the slice lands on the screen position of its world tile. Slices on the outer edge of the footprint stay open outwards, so overhanging geometry (a swinging ship's hull) is kept instead of cut; empty slices are not generated, and slices whose art is identical in all nine poses keep a single frame. There is no whole-ride `view-<w>` sprite for these rides.
- Multi-tile track pieces are clipped into tile-sized cells so renderers can preserve tile painter order. Model rotations change the camera view while keeping the lighting direction fixed; frame alignment preserves a shared anchor across animation poses. Guest frames are ordered by tint, cardinal direction, then walking pose; staff frames use role and direction in their key. The runtime combines entity heading with camera rotation and selects a walking pose from simulation ticks. Guest appearance and staff uniforms are model-generated, while the processed staff costume sheet remains a separate supplementary asset.
- Flower-bed generation takes the slope mask and camera view together. The soil and edging use the same diagonal split and corner heights as terrain rendering, while compact plants are sampled over that surface; generated slope variants therefore retain a tile-sized footprint without a raised planter wall. Flowerbed sprite output disables the model outline so adjacent tiles can meet without a dark outline seam. The runtime uses the generated native frame for both placed beds and ambient flowerbed state.
- Atlas packing trims transparent borders from native sprites and adjusts their anchors. Runtime placement treats the manifest anchor as the ground pivot, including the packer's trim compensation; the renderers share scenery and path-addition boxes through [src/render/ground-anchor.ts](../../src/render/ground-anchor.ts). Water remains a raw texture rather than a native anchored sprite, and its saturated blue palette is also recognized by [smoke/terrain-visual.spec.ts](../../smoke/terrain-visual.spec.ts).
- [ride-preview.ts](ride-preview.ts) (`pnpm tsx tools/sprites/ride-preview.ts <out.png> <scale> <frame> <dirs> <ride-slug>...`) reassembles the slices on a tile grid for every camera rotation and ride orientation, for visual review.
