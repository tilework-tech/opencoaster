# Noridoc: Rendering

Path: @/src/render

### Overview

- Turns worker snapshots and map data into the isometric park view without reading simulation state.
- Uses WebGL2 for the primary renderer and Canvas2D as a fallback, with a shared multi-page sprite atlas.
- Shares projection and picking rules with the main-thread input layer.

### How it fits into the larger codebase

- [src/main.ts](../main.ts) selects the backend, supplies the current [snapshot](../worker/snapshot.ts) and [map data](../worker/map-data.ts), and requests draws from the browser frame loop.
- [renderer.ts](renderer.ts) defines the renderer boundary. [src/worker](../worker/docs.md) publishes render-ready map and snapshot data; renderers do not access worker-owned simulation state.
- Both backends use [ownership-overlay.ts](ownership-overlay.ts) to draw parcel state above the world canvas from map ownership flags: unowned tiles receive a translucent dark fill and dashed boundary, and sale tiles receive a land or rights marker. Owned land remains unmarked. This overlay is separate from terrain textures and the WebGL ground cache.
- [projection.ts](projection.ts) provides world projection and tile picking for rendering and build input. [create-renderer.ts](create-renderer.ts) handles WebGL setup and Canvas2D fallback.
- [src/input](../input/docs.md) preserves the world point under zoom and caps device pixel ratio before passing viewport dimensions to the renderer. The canvas is layered with DOM controls and overlays from [index.html](../../index.html).
- [tools/sprites](../../tools/sprites/docs.md) creates generated ride, facility, transport, coaster, path, water, and rock assets; [tools/art](../../tools/art/README.md) packs them before development and production builds.

### Core Implementation

- [sprite-pages.ts](sprite-pages.ts) owns the manifest and page images shared by both renderers. World and people categories load at startup; lazy categories load when a sprite key is resolved or a UI surface calls `requestCategory`. Until a category is ready, callers can draw their procedural fallback.
- [webgl2-renderer.ts](webgl2-renderer.ts) uploads each loaded atlas page as a nearest-filtered texture and batches native sprite instances in painter order. A draw binds at most eight page textures; when consecutive instances require more pages, the batch is split at that boundary and subsequent draws retain the original instance order. A small procedural atlas supplies fallback sprites. The DOM toolbar's separate UI sheet is outside the world atlas.
- [world-art.ts](world-art.ts) derives ride, facility, entrance/exit hut, path, water, terrain, scenery, path-addition, and litter keys from snapshot and map values. Flat rides select one of four views and use the idle frame or tick-driven operating animation; facilities and station huts select direction-facing variants. The park entrance uses the generated gate sprite. Rain shelters and vandalised benches, bins, and lamps select generated four-view sprites; regular lamps, flower beds, and gardens keep using prepared prop-sheet sprites.
- [ground-anchor.ts](ground-anchor.ts) is the shared placement contract for both renderers: it supplies ground boxes for scenery, flower beds (one tile wide, bottom edge on the tile's lower corner, with `sceneryFrameDrop` lowering the frame one level), and rain shelters, and path-addition edge selection (`pathAdditionEdges`: one piece per tile, on the lowest-numbered open edge, offset 96 world units from the tile centre), path-addition edge and ramp elevations, rotation-dependent tile-centre elevations for sloped scenery and flower beds, and the horizontal sprite-anchor shift used by WebGL2. The scenery centre elevation follows the diagonal shared by the two triangulated terrain faces for the camera rotation, rather than assuming the tile's base height.
- [coaster-art.ts](coaster-art.ts) maps tracked rides to coaster, water, or transport families and constructs track-cell and vehicle keys. [coaster-scene.ts](coaster-scene.ts) turns snapshot track and car layers into a reusable painter-ordered list; track-piece cells follow piece direction and camera rotation, coaster supports stack to the ground beneath the in-cell track sample nearest the tile centre (`supportOffset`, so a column stands under the rails even where a turn hugs a tile edge), and cars or boats use per-car poses. Water boat keys include each car's packed passenger count to select empty or loaded sprites; the meshes show up to four passenger figures for Log Flume boats and seven for River Rapids boats. Log Flume and River Rapids therefore render track and boats through this scene; their older static ride dioramas remain generated art but are no longer the ride's runtime representation.
- Generated guest sprites use the `people/guest-walk#N` frames from [tools/sprites](../../tools/sprites/docs.md), indexed by guest tint, heading, and walking pose. Both renderers resolve them from the eager `people` atlas; [GUEST_BOX](sprite-layout.ts) supplies the 20×24-pixel visibility and fallback bounds. Model-generated people frames render at native size. Guests in the `Riding` state (`isRidingGuestRow`) are skipped by both renderers: they are drawn by their ride instead. [coaster-scene.ts](coaster-scene.ts) seats up to four `people/rider-seated` figures along each coaster, mouse, railway and monorail car from the car's packed passenger count; Log Flume and River Rapids boats and chairlift chairs already bake riders into their car art, and flat and enclosed rides show none, so a rider is never also a walking sprite. Riders reappear as walkers at the exit when the guest leaves `Riding`. Generated staff sprites encode uniform role and cardinal facing in their keys and provide four walking poses; the renderers combine staff heading with camera rotation and cycle all four poses from simulation ticks. Canvas2D draws ambient trees, flower beds and placed scenery from the atlas using the catalog or shared boxes supplied by [sprite-layout.ts](sprite-layout.ts) and [ground-anchor.ts](ground-anchor.ts), matching WebGL2 sizing.
- WebGL2 and [canvas2d-renderer.ts](canvas2d-renderer.ts) consume the generated art. Canvas2D resolves the same page store through [canvas-atlas.ts](canvas-atlas.ts), draws sprites at manifest frame size and anchor, and keeps procedural terrain and fallback drawing. Both renderers use `pathAdditionGroundZ` for flat and ramp path additions, with ramp props anchored at the path centre elevation; `pathAdditionBox` gives intact and vandalised variants of a prop the same rendered dimensions. In WebGL2, ramp path-addition and flower-bed painter depth is adjusted from tile-base height to the same ground Z used to project each sprite, keeping visual placement and sorting elevation aligned. Benches, bins, and lamps are derived from path connection flags and rendered at every unconnected edge; each faces the path, rotates with the edge and camera, and follows the exact ramp height at that edge. Shelters remain centered on the path. The same helper output drives the translucent path-addition preview, so preview and completed props share position and sizing without adding save data. `PATH_ADDITION_BROKEN` selects the vandalised furniture variants. [src/ui/track-preview.ts](../ui/track-preview.ts) uses generated ride keys for build-placement ghosts.
- The [development art-coverage report](../../docs/art/coverage.md) exposes the sprite inventory and both backend paths; the Vite-only `/__art-coverage` endpoint renders it live. [tools/sprites/coverage.test.ts](../../tools/sprites/coverage.test.ts) verifies generated and prepared sprite-key availability for catalog entries, rejects ride rows with empty or missing keys, and checks fallback status when required art is missing. [canvas2d-renderer.test.ts](canvas2d-renderer.test.ts) also exercises the Canvas2D atlas path by drawing a rain shelter on a path tile.
- [src/render/coaster-scene.ts](coaster-scene.ts) marks rides whose coaster, water, or transport sprites are available so procedural track and vehicle markers are omitted for those rides. If the corresponding atlas art is not available, the renderer retains the procedural markers.
- Ground cache: the WebGL renderer paints terrain, boundary cliff faces and flat path fill into 512×512 world-pixel textures on demand by reusing the instanced sprite shader and its page-texture runs at zoom 1 ([ground-chunks.ts](ground-chunks.ts) provides `chunkRange` and the LRU cache). It draws map-boundary faces for all surfaces and exposed interior faces for rock tiles, using neighboring tile height as the face base. Chunk bounds for rock tiles include their extrusion to the ground plane; a changed tile also invalidates adjacent rock chunks because a shared face's height depends on both tiles. Chunks are keyed by coordinate and cleared on rotation, gridline toggle, or when the `world` atlas category finishes loading; a new `MapData` drops only chunks touching tiles whose height, surface, slope or water level changed. Ownership is drawn in its own overlay and does not invalidate ground chunks. The ownership overlay repaints only when the `MapData` identity or camera position, zoom, rotation, viewport size, or device-pixel ratio changes.
- Occluder pass: tiles that rise above the tiles behind them (computed per map and rotation) are re-submitted in the depth-sorted sprite pass together with animated water planes, so raised terrain still hides guests, scenery and water behind it despite the cached ground. Canvas2D queues occluders, paths and placed scenery separately alongside rides, vehicles, staff and guests, then depth-sorts all items by rotated world tile coordinates and terrain elevation in `Z_STEP` units. WebGL2 appends equivalent instances to its depth-sorted list; path depth uses the rotated tile centre and its elevation. Both paths use view-rotated coordinates for ordering.
- Sprite ordering uses [painter-order.ts](painter-order.ts) (stable counting sort) and `bufferSubData` uploads. [adaptive-resolution.ts](adaptive-resolution.ts) turns frame times into a backing-buffer scale; [src/app/bootstrap](../app/bootstrap/index.ts) applies it to the device pixel ratio passed to `Renderer.resize` and throttles HUD and panel DOM updates.

### Things to Know

- Atlas category names follow key prefixes in [tools/art/categories.ts](../../tools/art/categories.ts): `flat-rides`, `stalls`, `transport`, `track-<style>`, `vehicles`, `coaster-<family>`, and `water`. Coaster and water ride families have lazy pages; first use requests the matching category. WebGL's eight-page draw limit is handled by consecutive ordered batches, preserving painter order even when a view uses many ride categories.
- Native sprites are positioned with manifest frame dimensions and anchor metadata. The manifest anchor is the sprite's ground pivot. Atlas packing trims transparent borders asymmetrically, so WebGL2 applies vertical anchor compensation and [ground-anchor.ts](ground-anchor.ts)'s horizontal shift to preserve that pivot after trim; Canvas2D applies the corresponding shift for native and boxed sprites. Boxed sprites and their ghosts use the same unrounded destination-rectangle calculation, so preview sizing and anchor placement follow the placed art. Multi-tile coaster and transport track is represented as tile-sized cells so renderers can interleave it with other world content by painter depth.
- Terrain uses generated material surfaces and directional cliff sprites. WebGL draws exposed map-boundary faces for all terrain and interior faces for rock; Canvas2D draws rock faces at map boundaries and internal height drops. Both use `terrainEdgeFace` from [terrain-geometry.ts](terrain-geometry.ts) and rotate the directional cliff key with the camera. Canvas2D projects and clips the sprite onto the face quadrilateral, with a shaded fill when the atlas art is unavailable. Generated rock surface and cliff textures replace the prepared source rock keys. WebGL water is a planar surface below the recorded water level, lowered by `WATER_BANK.surfaceInset`; it is clipped against the same two terrain triangles per tile that the view's terrain quad uses (`terrainMeshTriangles`), and the fragment shader shades it by depth. Canvas2D clips against those same triangles so alternating corner heights keep separate submerged regions. Both renderers draw earthy bank faces from shared shoreline segments after each tile's water, so walls are never covered by their own tile's water. Both renderers select animation frames from the simulation tick.
- Footpath placement previews arrive through `Renderer.setGhosts` as `PathPieceGhost` entries. Both renderers draw them with the same sprite as a built piece (`pathElementSprite`), translucent, and at the same depth; refused tiles are tinted red. WebGL2 sends a per-instance ghost mode (accepted or refused) with the sprite; Canvas2D queues them per tile in the depth-sorted draw order and draws them through `drawGhostSprite`, so they sort correctly in every rotation.
- Ride, scenery, flowerbed, and path-addition placement previews arrive through `Renderer.setSpriteGhosts` as `SpriteGhost` entries. They are drawn translucent at the depth of the built piece and tinted red when refused. Scenery ghosts carry the catalog box and the slope- and camera-view-dependent tile-centre ground pivot used by placed scenery; the item's facing rotation remains a separate placement value. Flowerbed ghosts carry the shared bed box and ground pivot. Path-addition ghosts carry their open-edge world offset, edge ground level, rendered box, and optional source tile coordinates. WebGL2 appends these ghosts to the depth-sorted instance list, while Canvas2D groups them by source tile when supplied (falling back to their rendered tile coordinates) and enqueues them into its painter-order queue alongside completed objects; both backends apply the sprite's manifest anchor and packed-trim compensation.
- Placed scenery elements (`SmallScenery` from `map-data.ts`, including scenario trees) are drawn with their catalog sprite (`trees/...`, `scenery/...`). Both renderers use [ground-anchor.ts](ground-anchor.ts) to anchor them at the rendered tile-centre surface elevation, computed from the triangulated terrain and camera rotation, and to use the catalog's shared scenery box. The scenery placement tool supplies the tile slope and catalog item to the same ground-anchor contract, so the translucent preview uses the placed object's box, ground pivot and sprite anchor. Flower beds use a separate shared box and rotation-dependent tile-centre pivot for both their placed sprite and placement ghost. Their depth keys remain tied to the object's tile and elevation, ordered against terrain, paths, rides, vehicles, staff and guests using rotated tile-centre coordinates. For tree sprites, using an unrotated tile corner can tie or invert the order at certain view angles. Trees use the single-frame atlas sprite in all four rotations; the sprite is rotation-invariant, so only its projected position changes.
- Ambient trees are deterministic rendering decorations, not simulation entities or saved objects. Shared [world-art.ts](world-art.ts) selection keeps Canvas2D and WebGL2 aligned, selecting broadleaf/conifer variants on grass and palms or cacti on sand. Weather shading and rain are DOM overlays; worker snapshots provide the weather state.
- Ownership overlay coordinates use the same isometric projection, camera rotation, zoom, and CSS-pixel viewport as the map. It consumes map ownership and terrain height data, so the worker map message must be refreshed when map ownership changes. Non-owned tiles receive a dark translucent fill; the edge of owned territory gets a rail-and-post fence, while sale borders use gold dashes for land and blue dots for construction rights. Tiles offered for both show the combined gold treatment. The overlay is separate from ground chunks, so purchase changes repaint the overlay without invalidating terrain textures.
- [src/sim](../sim/core/docs.md) owns simulation behavior and placement validation. [smoke/docs.md](../../smoke/docs.md) describes browser-level rendering and build-control checks.
