# Noridoc: Simulation Map

Path: @/src/sim/map

### Overview

- Owns the deterministic tile map: terrain, water, ownership, walkable paths, and stacked map elements.
- Validates map edits and applies their economy charges through the map command boundary. Player-facing area tools (Land, Clear scenery, Water) each have a pure planner that the worker also uses for previews, so a preview and its command share one set of rules.
- Stores path surface style and scenery as map-element state, alongside bin, bench, rain shelter, and lamp additions on path elements.

### How it fits into the larger codebase

- [World](../core/world.ts) owns `TileMap`; [Simulation](../core/simulation.ts) applies [map commands](map-commands.ts) before the ordered simulation systems run.
- [Pathfinding](../pathfinding/docs.md) and guest/staff systems query walkability and connected path edges. Guests add trash/vomit using the map's path and bin queries; handymen locate and empty filled bins.
- Map arrays feed [world hashing](../core/hash.ts) and [save serialization](../../save/save.ts). `revision` is runtime metadata used by [worker map snapshots](../../worker/map-data.ts) and staff's cached bin index, so it is not persisted.
- The [UI](../../ui/docs.md) sends path style, queue-path, scenery, and path-addition placement as commands. Flower beds can also be represented in `World.grounds` for grass care; placeable flower-bed scenery is a separate map element.

### Core Implementation

- [tile-map.ts](tile-map.ts) stores shared terrain corner heights and derived tile heights/slopes, ownership and surface arrays, plus a fixed-capacity pool of stacked elements linked per tile. Ownership starts clear; `canBuild` accepts tiles marked as owned or with construction rights. `expansionOwnership` assigns the land-sale flag to the immediate ring outside an owned rectangle and construction-rights-only flags farther out; both the default terrain generator and scenario terrain generation use this helper after marking the outer map border unavailable. `arrays()` supplies persistent arrays to hashing and save code.
- [land-tool.ts](land-tool.ts) is the player-facing terrain planner. A `LandEdit` (raise, lower, flatten, smooth; a square of tiles; a corner mask for a single tile; an anchor tile for flatten) is evaluated against the whole `World`: owned tiles of the selection are edited (a selection with none is `NotOwned`), the changed vertices give the affected tiles and the price, and rides (flat-ride footprints and stalls), ground-level track, ride entrances and exits, the park entrance, and other tile objects refuse the edit with a `LandResult`. Small scenery on changed tiles is removed and its removal price added; a ground-level footpath is re-seated on flat or ramp ground and otherwise refuses. `planLand` leaves the world untouched and feeds the worker's `previewLand` reply; `applyLand` commits. Refusals reach the UI through `FeedbackDomain.Land`. `ownedSelection` (owned tiles of a square, or why there are none) and `structureConflict` (first ride, stall, entrance or exit tile in the way; track only when it runs near the old height) are exported for [area-tools.ts](area-tools.ts).
- [area-tools.ts](area-tools.ts) plans and applies the Clear scenery and Water tools. An `AreaEdit` is a square plus `tool: 'clear'` or `tool: 'water'` with a `lower` flag; `planArea` evaluates it without touching the world (the worker's `previewArea` reply) and `applyArea` commits, both returning an `AreaPlan` (result, refusal subject tile, price, owned selected tiles, changed tiles, target water level, removed-item count). It reuses `ownedSelection` and `structureConflict` from [land-tool.ts](land-tool.ts), so the square, ownership and structure rules match the Land tool and result codes are shared `LandResult` values.
  - Clear acts on owned tiles only; construction-rights-only and unowned tiles in the square are skipped, and a square with no owned tile is `NotOwned`. It removes every small-scenery element (each priced at its catalog `removalPriceCents`) and strips bin, bench, shelter and lamp flags (and the addition payload) from paths for free. Nothing to remove is `NoChange`; a total above cash is `InsufficientFunds`. No item refunds money.
  - Water keeps every connected body one flat sheet. `raiseStep` takes the selection's surface (its highest water, or its lowest ground when dry), adds one step, and floods outward over every neighbouring tile whose ground lies below the new surface, so water is bounded by banks and never stands above dry ground; it stops with `WaterSpills` when the body would exceed `MAX_WATER_BODY_TILES` (open ground below the surface), `NotOwned` when it reaches land the park does not own, and `WaterLevelClash` when it meets a lake whose surface is higher. `lowerStep` drops the whole connected sheet at the selection's highest surface by one step; tiles whose ground is at or above the new surface are stored dry (`0`). Each changed tile, including flooded tiles outside the selection, costs `WATER_TILE_ACTION_COST_CENTS`, and `AreaPlan.tiles`/`level` describe the resulting surface for the preview. Other refusals: `TooHigh` above `MAX_HEIGHT`, `TooLow` with no water, `RideInTheWay` / `EntranceInTheWay` from `structureConflict`, and `PathInTheWay` / `ObjectInTheWay` for any element a flooded tile carries below the new level (checked on the selection first so the refusal names what the player pointed at). `flattenWaterBodies` brings legacy stepped water to its lowest sheet level on load.
- [land.ts](land.ts) implements terrain, water, and ownership operations. `editTerrain` applies raw vertex moves for the land tool; rectangle raises select the lowest tiles and lowers select the highest; an edit plan simulates the operation, measures all actual vertex-height changes after neighbor propagation, and derives the affected tiles. Terrain edits preserve the one-level neighbor slope invariant and roll back when an element would be buried or invalidly tilted. The map-command boundary prices the changed height units and removes litter from every affected tile.
- [paths.ts](paths.ts) places/removes flat or one-level ramp path elements. `planPathPlacement` runs the checks without changing the map and returns a `PathPlan`; `placePathOutcome` applies it, so the UI preview and the placement share one set of rules. `previewPathRun` applies a batch in order to `TileMap.clone()` and reports each piece's final state; the `placePathRun` command applies the same batch to the live map, so a laid run matches its preview. A placement without an explicit slope follows the terrain: flat ground gets a flat piece, a one-level slope gets a ramp facing its rise, and any other shape is refused as `UnevenGround`. `placePathOutcome` returns a `PathResult` naming the refusal; `placePath` is its boolean form. It recomputes edge connectivity, and attaches one of `Bin`, `Bench`, `Shelter`, or `Lamp` flags to the lowest path selected by the path query. Bin fill is stored as one count on that path element; the four-corner bin occupancy model is deferred. Trash deposited in a bin accumulates up to its capacity, and a handyman empties it.
- Path elements persist a surface style (tarmac, crazy paving, dirt) in `TileMap.pathStyle`; queue paths use a path flag and stay distinct from style.
- [scenery.ts](scenery.ts) defines placeable trees, shrubs, gardens, and fences; checks rotation, bounds, ownership/build rights, dry land, and element clearances; and stores the item and rotation as a small-scenery element. `placeScenery`/`removeScenery` commands charge the landscaping ledger with project-tuned (not original) prices.
- [map-commands.ts](map-commands.ts) validates affordability and ownership for terrain commands, validates affordability for land, water, and path edits, records charges in the ledger, and routes path-addition commands. Terrain planning counts propagated changes before applying an edit; `setTileHeight` also requires the tile to be owned. Ownership distinguishes owned land, construction rights, and land or rights offered for sale; buying a rectangle acquires only matching sale tiles. The `raiseWater` / `lowerWater` / `clearScenery` commands run `applyArea` and charge `costCents` to the landscaping ledger on success; refusals are reported through `FeedbackDomain.Water` or `FeedbackDomain.Clear` (with `LandResult` codes). The older absolute `setWater` command, which sets a rectangle to a given level, remains. Prices are named fixed constants at present: terrain changes cost 500 cents per changed height unit, water changes cost 250 cents per changed tile (for both `setWater` and Water tool steps), land costs 10,000 cents per tile and construction rights 4,000 cents per tile. Land purchase controls live in [the UI](../../ui/docs.md); scenario-configured land prices remain unimplemented. Path additions currently have no separate charge. A footpath removal credits `PATH_REFUND_CENTS`, equal to the placement cost. `previewPathPlacement` reports the outcome a placement would get, including the cash check, without changing the map. `previewScenery` does the same for a scenery spot: `sceneryPlacementResult` in [scenery.ts](scenery.ts) holds the placement rules (ownership, water, clearance) that `placeScenery` also applies, so a ghost and its placement agree. The `placeScenery` command takes the piece's rotation (0-3), which is stored on the element.
- `surface` is a derived draw class; path elements remain the source of path connectivity and height. Elevated pieces can coexist with lower elements where clearances permit.

### Things to Know

- Stored simulation values are integers and map state is deterministic. Do not write derived tile heights/slopes directly; update terrain through its operations so neighboring tiles stay consistent.
- Tile elements are pool slots, not stable IDs. A path addition modifies flags/data on a particular path element; `setPathAddition` requires an in-bounds buildable tile with a path. Vandalism marks a bench, bin, or lamp broken in a reserved payload bit while retaining its addition and contents; placing the path piece again clears the broken state. Broken bins do not accept litter and are not handyman emptying targets.
- Bins collect trash; benches, bins, and lamps can be marked broken by guest vandalism. Shelter additions are stored on paths, but no current guest system applies a shelter effect. These behaviors are implemented by [guests](../guests/docs.md) and [staff](../staff/docs.md), not by map storage itself.
- Flower beds and grass care live in `World.grounds` and are handled by the staff grounds system; they are not path additions or part of the map arrays. [scenery-effects.ts](scenery-effects.ts) provides a clipped 10×10 scan of small/large scenery and broken paths for guest surroundings; litter is supplied by the guest system. Fountain/music effects and scenario-configured land prices remain outside this module. See [world mechanics](../../../docs/mechanics/world.md) for researched behavior and project tuning.

### Queue lines

- A queue piece is a path element with `PathFlag.Queue`. Queue pieces join other queue pieces on every side, join an ordinary path only at the line's open end, and never join along their length ([paths.ts](paths.ts) `queueOpening`). A piece is an open end when it has at most one queue neighbour and is not a ride entrance already joined to a line; it joins one ordinary neighbour, preferring the one straight on from its queue neighbour.
- `elData` bits `PATH_ENTRANCE_MARK` and `PATH_EXIT_MARK` record the path tiles that hold a ride's entrance and exit. The [ride queue sync](../rides/queue-lines.ts) writes them (they are saved with the map); the exit mark stops queues joining the exit tile, and placing a normal piece over an entrance (or a queue piece over an exit) is refused. Because an opening depends on its neighbours, `recomputePathEdges` refreshes tiles two steps away.
- `TileMap.edgeMask` (the pathfinder's view) hides queue tiles from the walking network; `pathEdges` keeps the joined edges the renderer and the queue walk use. `previewPathRun` also reports `QueueLink` bits per queue piece: whether its line reaches a ride entrance and whether its open end joins a path.
