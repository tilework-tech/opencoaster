# Noridoc: Vehicles

Path: @/src/sim/vehicles

### Overview

- Advances train-level vehicle state along registered track polylines using fixed-tick integer physics.
- Collects test-run metrics and writes provisional tracked-ride ratings back to the ride store.
- Adapts cached track-system paths into fixed-point vehicle input and retains a small synthetic fixture boundary for isolated simulation tests.

### How it fits into the larger codebase

- [`vehicleSystem`](./vehicle-system.ts) is registered in [`SYSTEM_ORDER`](../core/simulation.ts) and advances the [`World`](../core/world.ts) vehicle store on each simulation tick.
- The vehicle system refreshes `World.vehicleTracks` from [`getTrackPath`](../track/track-path.ts) when a ride's track version changes. The registry is derived runtime state and is not part of the typed-array world state or its save/hash columns.
- [`createVehicleTrackFromPath`](./vehicle-track.ts) maps track piece events/modes, roll orientation, bank, and sampled tangent changes into vehicle segment metadata. Tests can still provide synthetic polylines directly to isolate physics.
- Train state references rides by stable ride ID. On test completion, ratings are written to the ride store only if that ride ID is still alive.
- Vehicle coordinates are available in the world snapshot path for rendering; renderers consume snapshots rather than reading simulation stores directly, as described in [`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`](../../../docs/ARCHITECTURE.md).

### Core Implementation

- [`VEHICLE_SCHEMA`](./vehicle-store.ts) stores one struct-of-arrays row per train. Progress and speed use signed Q8 fixed-point values; the row also holds dispatch and composition fields, physics state, ride-test measurements, and excitement/intensity/nausea ratings. [`spawnTrain`](./vehicle-store.ts) initializes a row and [`getTrainState`](./vehicle-store.ts) exposes a snapshot-shaped read model.
- [`createVehicleTrack`](./vehicle-track.ts) accepts integer XYZ polyline points, per-segment flags, optional signed Q8 lateral and vertical curvature, Q8 bank sine/cosine, inverted-orientation metadata, block-open metadata, and loop/crashability settings. Inversion flags count inversion events; the separate orientation array determines the sign of vertical G while the train is inverted. Bank metadata lets the force calculation adjust lateral G. It validates segment metadata and nonzero segment lengths; loop inputs must end at their starting point. It then precomputes integer segment lengths, cumulative Q8 arclength, block-section starts, and each drop's height from its local preceding crest through the continuous descent. The registry provides stable ride-ID lookup and a tick-ordered track list.
- [`stepVehicles`](./vehicle-system.ts) performs deterministic fixed-tick updates in vehicle-slot order. Acceleration and drag are integrated over the time spent on each segment; when a tick crosses boundaries, it carries remaining tick time forward and processes segment controls and entry events in order. Motion combines slope gravity, inverse-mass rolling friction, speed drag, and controls for chain lift, launch, brakes, and blocks. Final XYZ coordinates are interpolated directly into SoA columns without allocating a temporary tuple.
- Waiting trains dispatch from station segments after the minimum wait and either a full load or the maximum wait (rides override this with their own rules). Passenger counts and capacity are inputs to this condition; this area does not load or unload guests.
- `VehicleSegmentFlag.StationDrive` is set on every segment of a station piece and gives a train a 512 Q8 minimum speed, so it rolls out of the station. `VehicleTrack.stationSegment` is the first station segment, `stationEndQ8` / `stationEndSegment` the end of the platform. On loop tracks a Running train entering the station becomes `Arriving`: its speed is held under the braking curve `sqrt(2 * decel * remaining)` (with a small creep speed so friction cannot stop it short) and it halts exactly at the platform end in `Unloading`. The ride then owns the timer (`unloadTicks`) and sets `Waiting`. A Testing train entering the station still completes the lap instantly and records its ratings.
- [ride-trains.ts](./ride-trains.ts) (`spawnRideTrain`, `findRideTrain`, `rideTrainSlots`, `removeRideTrains`) is the interface the [rides](../rides/docs.md) coaster system uses to own the trains of a coaster. A train has `ceil(capacity / 4)` cars (at most five, so it fits the platform), each `CAR_MASS` (1800) in mass; riders add `PASSENGER_MASS` (80) each. `minWaitTicks = 0xffff` so trains never self-dispatch; the ride decides when to dispatch. A `Waiting` train is placed at the platform end, a `Testing` train at the platform start.
- A block section admits one train at a time. Adjacent segments marked as blocks share one occupancy reservation. Closed or occupied sections hold an arriving train at the boundary; an open block applies a crawl-speed cap while any car is on it.

#### Block zones

On loop tracks the end of the station and the end of every block-brake run are boundaries; zone k is the stretch after boundary k-1 up to boundary k (`VehicleTrack.boundariesQ8`). A train occupies the zones of its front and of its rear (`carCount * CAR_LENGTH_Q8` behind). A Running train brakes to stop exactly at the next boundary while another train of the same ride occupies the zone beyond it, and released from a hold it leaves at crawl speed. With Z boundaries at most Z-1 trains can run (`maxTrainsOnTrack`), matching the spec's "n blocks, n-1 trains". `stationDepartureClear`, `stationZoneClear` and `trainHeldAtBoundary` are the queries the ride system uses. Zones need a loop with at least two boundaries; otherwise only the legacy per-run block reservation applies.

#### Per-car forces

A train is a rigid chain of `carCount` cars `CAR_LENGTH_Q8` apart; one speed and head position describe it, but the forces are summed per car: slope acceleration is the mean over the cars' own segments, and a lift, brake, block or station drive acts while any car is on it. A long train therefore feels a whole hill and keeps being pulled over a crest by its rear cars. Cars do not have independent state: their positions are derived from the head (`carSegment`), which keeps the per-tick cost to a segment lookup per car (a full simulation tick with a two-train, three-car coaster runs in about 0.03 ms mean). Per-car G-forces and per-car crash geometry are not modelled; forces are sampled at the head as in the spec.

#### Friction

Rolling friction is `16 * 1024 / (mass + passengers * PASSENGER_MASS)` in Q10 (quarter Q8 steps) and is applied with unbiased deterministic rounding (`frictionDither`, derived from tick, train and slice), so heavy trains lose a fraction of a unit per tick instead of the old fixed minimum of one. There is no mass floor hack: ride trains get their mass from their cars.

- Collision checks require trains on the same ride to make contact or close the gap within one tick, as well as meeting the combined-speed threshold. For loop tracks, the forward gap is selected from the faster train's direction of travel and wraps across the start/end seam, which is geometrically closed at track creation. Crest and drop-start crash thresholds are separate and apply only when the track is marked crashable; that setting defaults to false. The defaults are 50 and 64 Q8 world-speed units, intended to approximate the spec's 50 and 64 km/h targets; unit conversion and calibration remain open. Test-run stalls can also transition a train to the stalled state.
- Testing trains collect speed, distance, elapsed ticks, drops, drop height, inversions, vertical/lateral force extrema, and airtime. Force extrema sample every segment crossed during a tick, and maximum speed tracks intermediate boundary peaks. Vertical gravity changes sign on segments marked inverted; banked lateral G uses the fixed-point lateral load multiplied by bank cosine and subtracts bank sine. Reaching the end of a non-loop test path completes the run, computes ratings, and writes them to the associated ride.
- [`calculateTrackedRideRatings`](./vehicle-ratings.ts) applies shared force, drop, inversion, and lateral-force threshold terms, plus named original additions for average/max speed, track length, and airtime. Intensity thresholds reduce excitement by the provisional 0.75 factor recorded in [`docs/DECISIONS.md`](../../../docs/DECISIONS.md).

### Things to Know

- The fixed-point and deterministic rules come from the simulation architecture: no wall clock or random source is used in vehicle stepping, and track lengths/curvature metadata are prepared outside the per-tick path.
- Track flags describe simulation events and controls, not editable track pieces. Track editing, ride-mode configuration beyond the stored dispatch fields, and guest loading/unloading are not implemented in this area; they belong to track and rides.
- Real track paths expose station, chain-lift, brake, block-brake, and launch controls: `PieceMode.Launch` maps to the existing Launch segment flag, so placed launch pieces activate the launch physics.
- Ride types with track rules allow one station piece per ride, so the first station is the only one in practice.
- The path adapter estimates signed curvature from sampled tangents (with `curvatureShift = 6` extra fractional bits so gentle curves are not rounded to zero; hand-built tracks keep plain Q8) and uses the track path's integer-degree bank samples.
- Force sampling works in the rider's frame: the vertical-plane and horizontal curvature (`normalG`, `sideG`) are rotated by the bank angle, so `up = normal * cos(bank) + side * sin(bank)` and `lateral = side * cos(bank) - normal * sin(bank)`. Banked turns therefore load the seat by about `1 / cos(bank)`, and a corkscrew (bank sweeping through 180 degrees) swings gravity from seat to side to head without any inverted flag. The loop still uses `invertedSegments` because its bank stays at zero. Calibration against the spec's 2.8 G table with the game speed scale (1 world unit per tick is about 5.1 km/h): small turn 58 km/h (spec 59), S-bend 100 (101), corkscrew 68 (71), banked small turn 80 (101) at a 45 degree bank. With the 60 degree bank the banked small turn is estimated at about 95 km/h and the medium at about 162; these two are analytic estimates from the lateral-G formula, not measured in the simulation.
- Speed to km/h conversion and the crash thresholds are still uncalibrated beyond that table.
- Ratings are provisional. Drop height enters in height steps of 32 world units (a track level is two steps, as in the spec). The shared force/drop/inversion terms follow the public-source formulas summarized in [`docs/mechanics/track.md`](../../../docs/mechanics/track.md); speed and airtime additions are original design terms, and length is included as a bounded original term. Ride-type-specific coefficients and calibration against play are not yet available here, so these values should be treated as initial tuning rather than a claim of original-game parity.
- Train and ride rating columns are included in [`hashWorld`](../core/hash.ts)'s live entity-store hashing, so rating writeback is part of determinism comparisons.
- G-force values and speed metrics use integer fixed-point storage, and ride ratings are stored as integers. Curvature-based acceleration is divided by the model gravity and converted to Q8 G before it contributes to the ride-test force extrema. Consumers should use the schema/API rather than infer floating-point units from field names.
- Synthetic polyline tests establish deterministic vehicle behavior independently of track construction. The vehicle system is inert when `World.vehicleTracks` is absent.
