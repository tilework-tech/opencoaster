# Noridoc: Guests

Path: @/src/sim/guests

### Overview

- Stores guest state in typed struct-of-arrays columns and advances guest behaviour through the `guests` simulation system.
- Guests arrive at the park gate, walk the real footpath network with the [pathfinder](../pathfinding/docs.md), use rides, stalls and toilets, pay through the [economy](../economy/docs.md), and leave through the gate.
- Litter has separate store and system entry points in this folder; guests periodically create trash or vomit on walkable tiles.

### How it fits into the larger codebase

- [World](../core/world.ts) owns the guest and ride stores, the map, the economy ledger and `world.park` (the gate tile, [park.ts](park.ts)). [Simulation](../core/simulation.ts) runs `guestSystem` in the deterministic system order and routes `spawnGuests` and `setParkEntrance` here.
- Movement calls `nextDirection` from [pathfinding](../pathfinding/navigate.ts) at every tile centre, passing the target tile (a ride access tile or the gate) or `-1` to wander. `heading` stores the last direction so the pathfinder avoids reversing. Guests never leave the footpaths: positions advance 12 units per update toward the next tile centre.
- Destinations are ride-store entries (rides, stalls, toilets alike) addressed by `targetRide`. A destination is only chosen when `isReachable` says a footpath route to its access tile exists. For queue rides that is the placed entrance. For stalls and facilities it is the walk node (tile plus path layer) of the footpath piece that serves the window, from `stallServiceNode` ([rides](../rides/docs.md#stall-storefronts)); a stall whose front nothing serves returns -1 and is never chosen, even while open. Arrival compares the guest's own walk node with that node, so a guest on a bridge stacked over the front tile has not arrived.
- The [ride queue system](../rides/queue-system.ts) seats `Waiting` guests when a ride dispatches; this system finishes the ride when the ride's completed-cycle counter moves on.
- Money: gate fees post to `Admission`, tickets to `RideTickets` (at boarding, only in per-ride price mode), food and drink to `FoodDrinkSales` with a stock cost to `FoodDrinkStock`, and toilet fees and successful umbrella kiosk purchases to `ShopSales`. Arrival chance reads `parkRating` and the active marketing campaign from the economy. Campaigns can also apply an arrival voucher; entry vouchers change the gate fee, and free ride or food vouchers waive the fare or price once at their named ride or stall ([vouchers.ts](vouchers.ts)). A ride-targeted campaign assigns new arrivals to its selected ride while that ride is open. Willingness to pay ([willingness.ts](willingness.ts)): a guest refuses a ticket above twice the ride's value (never less than $6), and at a stall compares the price with the item's value (`itemValueCents` in the ride catalog, falling back to its starting price): the overpricing is halved above 128 happiness and halved again above 180, and a guest who rolls 0 to 7 tenths of a dollar tolerates that much. Refusals record a "not paying that much" thought; information kiosks and map sales are exempt.

### Core Implementation

- [Guest store](guest-store.ts): states `Wandering`, `Queuing` (in a ride's queue line: single file, shuffling toward the entrance, or, with no destination, walking back out to the open end), `Riding`, `Leaving`, `Shopping` (using a stall or toilets from the footpath in front of its window) and `Waiting` (at the head of a ride queue, counted in the ride's `queueLength`, or waiting for a busy stall) and `Sitting` (walking to, resting on, or rising from a bench seat; see Bench resting). The store also holds the rest columns `seat`, `benchGoal` and `restCooldown`, and owns `GUEST_UPDATE_STRIDE`, the four-tick stagger shared with the resting code. `spawnGuest` places a guest at a configured map-edge point; when no edge spawn is configured, the gate is the legacy fallback. If neither is available, creation returns false and the caller stops adding guests; its wallet uses the scenario average plus the OpenRCT2 reference offsets of -$10, $0, +$10, or +$20. The starter's configured average is $50. These values are source-derived OpenRCT2 compatibility references, not universal RCT2 settings. [Scenario setting](../scenario/scenarios/starter.json), [OpenRCT2 Park.cpp](https://github.com/OpenRCT2/OpenRCT2/blob/develop/src/openrct2/world/Park.cpp)
- [Guest system](guest-system.ts) updates each guest on a stable four-tick stagger. Needs change every 128 world ticks: hunger satisfaction falls by 2, toilet need rises by 1, nausea falls by 2, and thirst satisfaction falls by 1 at temperatures of 21 C or higher. Idle motives update every 512 ticks (four need updates): energy falls by 1 (never below 32), the happiness target drifts by 1 toward 127, low energy or needs can reduce it by up to 5, and actual happiness moves by up to 4 toward that target. Ride satisfaction and positive surroundings raise the target. At tile-centre decisions, guests may seek facilities, food or drink, a rain kiosk, or a ride. On each idle-motive update a wandering guest decides whether to leave, as in [OpenRCT2's `GuestDecideWhetherToLeavePark`](https://github.com/OpenRCT2/OpenRCT2/blob/develop/src/openrct2/entity/Guest.cpp): with energy of at least 55, happiness of at least 45 and cash of at least 500 cents it stays; otherwise it leaves with about a 5% chance per check, whether or not it stands at a tile centre. A guest with no rides taken and at least 10,240 visit ticks first runs normal ride selection; it heads for the ride it finds and, when none is eligible, loses 128 from its happiness target and leaves. Every 1,024 ticks a guest raises one randomly chosen felt need as a thought: tired (energy 70 or less and happiness under 128), hungry (satiation 10 or less), thirsty (25 or less), needs toilet (160 or more), or out of money (cash under 500 while happy and rested). A repeated thought moves to the newest slot instead of duplicating. The idle-motive cadence follows OpenRCT2's slower motive update gate.
- Stalls and toilets are noticed within 10 tiles (Chebyshev), which realises the decision "head to a stall it has seen, otherwise wander and buy on encounter": a stall on the guest's own tile has distance 0. There is no per-guest memory of seen stalls.
- Ride choice ([guest-system.ts](guest-system.ts) `scoreRide`) works in ride units (hundredths). [preferences.ts](preferences.ts) converts the guest's 0-255 preferred-intensity byte and one of four nausea-tolerance buckets (none, low, average or high): a ride above the guest's intensity ceiling (preferred + 1.50) is refused with a "too intense" thought, a ride too sickening for the guest's tolerance with "too nauseous", and one whose line is full with "queue too long". Eligible rides score `4 x excitement - 2 x intensity gap - 3 x nausea - price - 100 x line / capacity - rain penalty + taste`; the capacity-normalized line cost lets modest queues stay attractive while making a nearly full line less attractive. A ride is filtered when queued plus approaching guests reach capacity. `taste` is a small deterministic hash of guest, ride and a 2,048-tick epoch. [docs/BALANCE.md](../../../docs/BALANCE.md) lists the scoring terms.
- Lines: [ride-lines.ts](ride-lines.ts) recounts, at the start of every guests update, per ride slot the guests `queued` (Queuing or Waiting), `approaching` (Wandering with that ride as destination) and `serving` (Shopping). The counts live on `world.rideLines`, are derived from guest state, and are neither saved nor hashed, so a loaded game recounts to the same values. A ride's line capacity is `rideQueueCapacity` (queue tiles + 1, four guests each); the combined queued and approaching count filters out a full ride, and arrivals are turned away at capacity. `queued` measures guests physically in the queue or waiting at its entrance; `approaching` guests still travel over public paths. Stable guest IDs decide which same-ride arrival can remain at a blocked join; later arrivals turn back from the path-side approach so they do not remain parked at the entrance. The worker snapshot reports the physical `queued` count as the ride's queue length.
- [ride-demand.test.ts](ride-demand.test.ts) exercises a 1,000-guest crowded park with a free gentle ride and a 56-person queue for 8,000 ticks. In the observed run, the physical queue peaked at 38 guests, the combined queued-plus-approaching count reached capacity, and the final sample showed 28 queued plus 28 approaching. No guest stayed at the same position on an adjacent public path across three 100-tick samples; the test bounds this stationary count at 12. Up to 69 guests still moved through the measured nearby public-path area, so this check bounds stationary loitering rather than total path traffic.
- Queue lines: a guest only enters a line after choosing that ride. It paths over the ordinary network to the tile where the line's open end joins the path (queue tiles are not part of the walking network, see [pathfinding](../pathfinding/docs.md)), checks the line is open and has room (`rideQueueCapacity`; otherwise it thinks "queue too long" and wanders off), and steps onto the open-end tile as `Queuing`. A queuing guest walks down the line's chain (see [queue lines](../rides/docs.md)) until it stands one `QUEUE_SPACING` (64 units, four to a tile) behind the guest ahead, so the line is single file, turns corners with the chain, and shuffles forward when the head is served. It faces along the queue and idles (the renderers show a slow weight shift while a guest stands still). Patience counts up while it waits: after 2,400 ticks it thinks "I've been queuing for ages" and loses happiness; past `2,400 + 24 x happiness` ticks it gives up, turns around and walks out to the open end (outbound guests do not block the line). When the ride closes or breaks down everyone in the line walks out the same way. The first `capacity` guests of the line, once standing in place and with everyone ahead already waiting, become `Waiting` (still walking up as guests ahead board) and board lowest `queueSeq` first. Litter is not dropped on queue tiles because staff do not walk them.
- Rides entered straight from a path (old saves) keep this older behaviour: a guest arriving at the entrance tile becomes `Waiting` and increments `queueLength`, which still counts only the guests available to board. An unhappy guest (happiness at most 65) can balk from a thrill-ride queue after 4,300 ticks, with a 3.3% chance at each 128-tick check. On dispatch the queue system turns that many `Waiting` guests (lowest slot first) into `Riding`, stamping the low 16 bits of the ride's `cyclesCompleted` in `activityTicks`. When the counter changes the guest steps onto the ride exit tile (or the entrance when the exit is not a footpath), gains happiness (`8 + excitement / 40`) and energy, and starts a ride cooldown. Demolition moves guests still targeting that ride to its walkable exit, entrance, or nearest walkable map tile; when the map has none, guests retain the ride coordinates. It clears the ride target and navigation progress and returns them to `Wandering`. Guests remember their last ride and ride count; repeating the same ride requires the audited happiness, energy, nausea, satiation and toilet checks plus a random gate.
- Stalls and facilities serve `capacity` guests at a time for `runTicks` each. A guest arriving at a busy one becomes `Waiting` at its access tile (without touching `queueLength`) and starts shopping as soon as a place is free, or gives up after the queue patience. Food and drink sales post the descriptor's per-item stock cost separately from sale income; guests have no fixed number of purchases per visit. Guests skip stalls whose line is eight or longer. [OpenRCT2 shops and stalls](https://docs.openrct2.io/en/latest/playing/shops_and_stalls/), [OpenRCT2 ShopItem.cpp](https://github.com/OpenRCT2/OpenRCT2/blob/develop/src/openrct2/ride/ShopItem.cpp)
- Patience: 2,400 ticks to reach a destination; stalls and facilities have a 3,200-tick wait timeout. Walking to a destination refreshes approach patience whenever the guest gets closer to its entrance than it has been (`approachGap`). Thrill-ride queues use the probabilistic balk check above. A guest whose ride closes drops the destination.
- Arrivals: each simulation tick with a gate, `guest-system.ts` compares one 16-bit random value with a base rate of `50 + clamp(parkRating - 200, 0, 650)`, then applies marketing and award attraction factors. When guest count exceeds the sum of open rides', stalls' and facilities' descriptor generation bonuses, the rate is quartered; the sensible starter's seven open types contribute 200. An entry fee quarters the rate when it exceeds the combined open-ride value for money (ride value net of ticket prices); if half the fee still exceeds that value, the rate is quartered again. `spawnArrival` creates the guest only when the park has an edge spawn or legacy gate fallback, then charges the fee. Initial population uses the same spawn-point selection through `spawnGuest`. Only combined open-ride value for money is cached, refreshing on initialization and every 512 ticks; park rating, entry fee, marketing, award and voucher inputs are read on every arrival roll. These rates and descriptor values are OpenRCT2 compatibility references, not values specified by the RCT2 manual. [OpenRCT2 Park.cpp](https://github.com/OpenRCT2/OpenRCT2/blob/develop/src/openrct2/world/Park.cpp), [OpenRCT2 RideData.cpp](https://github.com/OpenRCT2/OpenRCT2/blob/develop/src/openrct2/ride/RideData.cpp)
- Weather: guests without an umbrella receive a ride-score penalty for thrill and coaster rides during rain or storms, and in rain walk to the nearest affordable open kiosk and buy an umbrella there (price to `ShopSales`; ownership is stored by stable guest id). A wandering guest can also buy from an open kiosk within one tile; both purchase routes increment that kiosk's `guestsServed` counter. Rain does not directly reduce guest happiness, and path shelters currently have no guest weather behavior. [surroundings.ts](surroundings.ts) periodically assesses wandering guests: it counts small and large scenery plus nearby litter and broken paths in a clipped 10-by-10 window, records a scenery or cleanliness thought, and adds the named happiness bonus. Each guest is assessed once per 9,216 ticks (18 idle-motive periods). The map-side scenery index is cached by map revision.
- Park interior: [park-interior.ts](park-interior.ts) `isParkInterior` allows mess only on owned land (construction rights alone and unowned land do not count) that is not on the gate approach. `gateApproachMask` runs breadth-first traversal over path graph node IDs and marks the gate plus the shortest connected route to each spawn; when a spawn node is disconnected from the gate node it falls back to the straight axis-aligned span. The traversal reads each node's own edge mask and ignores invalid step results before enqueueing them, preserving the graph's per-layer connectivity for stacked paths. The resulting mask is still per tile: if a bridge and lower path share a tile on the route, both layers are classified outside, so this does not independently classify every bridge arrangement. Other connected path tiles stay interior. Where two routes are equally short the search picks one deterministically. The mask is cached per world and rebuilt only when `map.pathVersion`, the gate or the spawn points change, so per-guest checks are one array lookup. Editing paths so the route changes updates classification but does not purge existing litter; `purgeExteriorMess` runs after a successful entrance change or save load. A guest's current tile decides eligibility: arrivals remain outside until they step past the gate, and leaving guests remain in the park until they reach the gate; the gate tile itself is ineligible. `dropLitter` and vandalism refuse ineligible tiles. The bridge route regression uses a real map with ramps and a stacked crossing whose lower path has different exits; it verifies route connectivity and that a nearby lower-only path tile remains interior.
- Litter: [litter-system.ts](litter-system.ts) checks every 160 ticks. Guests above the nausea threshold may create vomit and lose nausea and hunger; otherwise each guest has a one-in-128 chance to create trash. Trash can fill an unbroken bin's four corner counters; if no bin has room, trash is stored as a litter entity. Vomit always uses the litter store. The store caps litter entities at 500 and evicts the newest item when full. Terrain edits remove litter from affected tiles.
- Leaving guests walk to the gate with the same pathfinder and despawn on reaching it. With no gate at all they despawn at once. A leaving guest with a gate it cannot reach stays in the park and wander, thinks "I'm lost!" and loses 30 happiness every 11,520 ticks (O-44's 90 passes of 128 ticks) instead of vanishing. A guest that is not leaving but cannot reach the gate or any destination leaves after the stranded visits above.
- Stranded guests: a guest with no reachable destination whose footpath has no route to the gate is lost (it wanders and thinks "I'm lost!") and counts each tile-centre visit in `navSteps`. After `STRANDED_LEAVE_VISITS` (40) visits it leaves the park. A visit that finds a route to the gate resets the count. The park's lost-guest count feeds a news warning once five or more guests are lost.
- Lost guests ([navigation.ts](navigation.ts)): `noteNavigation` runs after each goal-directed step. A step whose pathfinder search saw the target, or that brought the guest closer (tile gap) than it has been, resets `navSteps`; a blind choice that did not counts one; forced single-exit steps count for nothing. At `LOST_AFTER_CHOICES` (12, doubled with a map) blind choices the guest drops its destination, records the `Lost` thought and sets `lostTicks` to `LOST_WANDER_TICKS` (2,400): it wanders, picks no ride or stall, and heads for the nearest visible information kiosk if it has no map and can pay `MAP_PRICE` (60 cents). The kiosk sells it a map instead of an umbrella: `hasMap = 1`, lost state cleared, income to `ShopSales`. A map adds `MAP_JUNCTION_BONUS` (16) to the pathfinder's junction budget and doubles the blind-choice limit. Lost guests count in the park-rating "lost guests" input alongside guests standing off the footpaths. The trigger and the map's effect are placeholders (O-62 is unverified); `navGoal`, `navGap`, `navSteps`, `lostTicks` and `hasMap` are guest columns (save version 10).
- Energy starts at 65–128, is clamped to the original 32–128 range, and falls by 1 on each 512-tick idle-motive update; a ride restores 32 and sitting on a bench restores it too (see Bench resting). The guest window's energy bar spans 32–128. Low energy, low hunger/thirst satiation, and high toilet need also reduce the happiness target on that cadence.

### Things to Know

- Rates, thresholds, scores, patience and capacities are tunable design choices, not claims about the original game.
- Guests in `Riding` stand on the ride's footprint tile, which is not a footpath; consumers that check footpath occupancy (for example the lost-guest count in [park inputs](../economy/park-inputs.ts)) skip them.
- A guest whose tile is not a footpath (for example after the path under it is removed) cannot move; it eventually leaves when it is stranded and counts as lost for the park rating. Guest creation requires an edge spawn point or legacy park gate.
- `queueLength` counts only the guests at the head of the line who can board; the ride window and the wait estimate use the whole line (`rideLines.queued`). Capacity is enforced when a guest joins the line.
- Stalls serve any number of guests at once; `capacity` is not enforced. The Information Kiosk is selected for umbrellas in rain and also sells them to nearby wandering guests.
- Current saves use version 36 (v36 appends the Observation Tower height ride column). Version 35 appends the three rest columns; older saves load them as zeros (no one seated, no bench goal, no cooldown), and the seat claim map is rebuilt from the guests on load. Version 33 added map-edge guest spawn points to the park header; v32 saves and older fall back to the park gate, while v33 spawn points and existing guest positions are preserved when v34 materializes legacy ambient trees. New sandbox and scenario parks establish a walkable gate-to-edge approach before initial guests are queued. Other fields absent from older layouts receive defaults during load. Versions 3 and 4 also migrate guests with `HEADING_NONE`. See [save format and migrations](../../save/docs.md) for the full history.

### Bench resting

Benches were once purely decorative path additions; guests now sit on them. The behaviour is split between [benches.ts](benches.ts) (seats) and [resting.ts](resting.ts) (the sitting state machine), and is driven from [guest-system.ts](guest-system.ts).

- Seats: a usable bench is a flat, non-queue, non-boarding-gate path piece with the bench flag and no vandalism. It offers two seats on every edge not joined to another path, positioned between the tile centre and that open edge and spread along it, so a guest sits at the rendered bench and faces back toward the path. A seat is a code stored in the guest's `seat` column.
- Claims: `world.seatOwners` maps a seat to the guest holding it. It is derived state, validated lazily against the live guest on every lookup, never saved, and rebuilt from the guest columns by `rebuildSeatOwners` after load. Claiming picks a random usable edge to start from and the next usable one after it, and a random seat when both are free, like the reference.
- Bench lookup: benches are indexed in blocks of tiles that are rebuilt only when the map revision changes, so a nearest-free-bench search visits only the blocks overlapping its search square.
- Triggers: `wantsRest` is true at low energy (70 or less, the same line as the "tired" thought) or high nausea (128 or more). A wandering guest standing at a tile centre who wants to rest and has no cooldown sits on a bench on its own tile (the reference's pass-by case). Otherwise `chooseDestination`, after toilet, rain, food and drink needs and before rides, may call `seekBench`, which sets `benchGoal` to the nearest free reachable bench within 8 tiles (reachability through `isReachable`). Walking there gives up, with a long cooldown, when the need goes, patience runs out, the bench fills or disappears, or navigation fails. Sitting clears any ride target. Leaving, lost, queuing and riding guests never sit.
- Sitting: the guest walks to its seat at the usual walking step, stays for a duration derived from its energy (the reference's `(129 - energy) * 16 + 50` formula, or a nausea settle time, clamped to a fixed band), regains energy and sheds extra nausea each need period, then rises, walks back to the tile centre and resumes `Wandering` with a cooldown before its next rest. If the seat stops being usable (bench vandalised or removed, path removed, edge now joined to a path), the guest stands up at once. Picking a guest up releases its seat ([person-position.ts](../core/person-position.ts)).
- Adaptation versus the reference: OpenRCT2 triggers at energy 50 or less or nausea above 170 (or when holding food or drink while hungry or unhappy), only considers the bench on the tile being entered, never seeks one, and raises the energy target by 5 per 512 ticks. OpenCoaster's energy threshold of 70 sits above the sim's leave threshold (55) because rides restore energy; the nausea threshold, the local seeking within 8 tiles, the cooldowns, the recovery rates and the stand-up-when-invalid rule are OpenCoaster choices. The food and drink holding trigger is not implemented. See [guest fidelity audit](../../../docs/fidelity/guests.md).
- Observed usage: the nausea trigger is effectively unreachable in normal play (nausea stays far below 128 in the scripted park), so natural use comes from tired guests. In a headless six-month balance park with benches on every third eligible path tile, roughly one in nine guests sat at least once, for about 27 seconds on average. [bench-use.ts](../../../bench/bench-use.ts) measures this.

### Vandalism

- [path-sights.ts](path-sights.ts) `noteTileSights` runs when a wandering guest reaches a path tile centre. It keeps a 3-tile history of litter and of vomit (capped at 3 per tile) and a 6-tile history of vandalised additions in the packed `sightMemory` column; at a sum of 3 (or two vandalised tiles) a one-in-six roll records "bad litter", "path disgusting" or "vandalism" and lowers the happiness target by 17. Per-tile litter counts are cached against the litter store's count and newest birth order, and vandalised additions per tile against the map revision (skipped entirely while no addition is broken). See [staff mechanics](../../../docs/mechanics/staff.md).
- [vandalism.ts](vandalism.ts) runs every 32 ticks after staff. A wandering guest who is unhappy, energetic, and has recently stood on a tile containing litter or vomit may break a bench, bin, or lamp on their current path (never a bench that has a seated or approaching guest, a rule the reference also has); a security guard strictly less than seven tiles away (Chebyshev, world units) stops the attempt and is credited with a stopped vandal. Successful vandalism leaves the guest angry for a short period. That anger can sustain further attempts after the guest moves away from litter.
- A security guard with vandalism watch enabled within `SECURITY_RADIUS` (7 tiles, Chebyshev) deters the attempt. The nearest eligible guard receives a persisted stopped-vandal count; stable staff id breaks equal-distance ties. Broken additions retain their path flag and contents but are marked broken; broken bins neither accept trash nor qualify for handyman emptying. Replacing the addition clears its broken state. The simulation does not maintain an aggregate park-wide breakage counter.
